# Science

For every reported result, record:

1. What was measured, including units.
2. Which inputs were tested and how they were selected.
3. The number of independent samples.
4. The code, commit, and saved artifact that produced the result.
5. The strongest plausible alternative explanation.

Lay out observation, assumption, logic, and conclusion. Keep the claim limited
to the tested inputs and conditions. Correlation does not establish causation.
Synthetic data demonstrates code behavior, not performance on real inputs.
Report unexplained anomalies and unresolved results directly.

Review and revision may be collaborative. Never invent a number, omit a
material limitation, or convert exploratory evidence into a general claim.

## Complete when

- [ ] Every number has units and a stated meaning.
- [ ] Inputs and selection are recorded.
- [ ] Independent sample count is explicit.
- [ ] The result traces to code and saved output.
- [ ] The claim covers no more than what was tested.
- [ ] Alternative explanations and unresolved uncertainty are stated.
